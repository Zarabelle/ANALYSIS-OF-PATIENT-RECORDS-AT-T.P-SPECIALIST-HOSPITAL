This project shows how Excel can turn raw hospital records into clear, interactive insights for management reporting. The healthcare sector is data-driven and have data collected at different levels and in different ways to improve the quality of healthcare services and foster a healthy nation. The hospital is an important part of the nation and here, the majority of healthcare services are delivered. Data such as patient admissions and financial data are collected and analyzed in order to oversee general operational performance, track financial records, and inform management decision-making. In this project, the patient admissions, billing and insurance coverage records of patients in T.P Specialist Hospital between 2024 and 2025 will be analyzed in order to uncover useful insights embedded in it.  The dashboard gives decision-makers a single view of how many patients the hospital treats, which departments carry the most work, how much is billed and collected, and where attention is needed. ## 2. Dataset Overview (About)                         

The analysis uses a Hospital Patients dataset of **1,000 patient records** covering **2024 and 2025**.

It includes:

- Patient demographics (age group and gender)
- Admission dates
- Hospital departments
- Diagnoses
- Bill amounts and payment status
- Insurance coverage
- Length of stay
**Tools used**

- Microsoft Excel
- Formulas (IF, SUMIF, SUMPRODUCT)
- PivotTables and PivotCharts
- Slicers

**Methodology**

1. Reviewed and cleaned the dataset.
2. Created helper columns and formulas to classify records and total paid and pending bills.
3. Built PivotTables for each analysis area.
4. Created PivotCharts and connected slicers for Department, Year and Diagnosis.
5. Assembled everything into one dashboard with headline KPI cards.

## 3. Problem Statement

The hospital management needs a quick, reliable way to monitor admissions, to understand departmental workload, to track payments and also identify areas that need attention. Without a structured report, these patterns are hardly seem and thus, makes decision making slower.

This project answers said needs with an interactive dashboard built to address  management questions on visits, departments, billing, length of stay, payment status, outstanding amounts, insurance coverage, age groups, trends over time and areas needing attention.

## 5. Key Insights

- **Patient volume:** 1,000 patients were recorded. General has the highest count (252), followed by Emergency (190), Orthopedics (159), Pediatrics (153), Cardiology (152) and Neurology (94).
- **Age groups:** Adults are the largest group (527 patients), followed by Elderly (311), Children (111) and Teenagers (51).
- **Gender:** Female patients make up 51.20% and male patients 48.80%.
- **Payment status:** By bill value, 56.73% is fully paid, 17.41% is partially paid and 25.86% is pending. About $3.48 million of the $8.05 million billed is not yet fully paid.
- **Revenue by department:** Orthopedics and Cardiology generate the highest payment and insurance amounts, even though General treats more patients.
- **Length of stay:** Orthopedics has the longest average stay and Emergency the shortest.
- **Admission trend:** Monthly admissions fluctuate across 2024 and 2025 rather than following a steady rise, with a clear peak late in 2025.

## 8. Recommendations
The hospital managment needs to:
1. Strengthen follow-up on pending and partially paid bills to improve collection.
2. Match staffing and resources to departmental workload, especially General and Emergency.
3. Review Orthopedics, which combines long stays with high billing, for bed management and cost control.
4. Use monthly admission peaks to plan capacity and staffing ahead of busy periods.
5. Monitor insurance coverage by department and diagnosis to spot gaps in claims.
6. Continue regular KPI reporting through this dashboard.
7. Include a column to state partial payments made so far.

## 9. Conclusion

The T.P Specialist Hospitals Performance Dashboard shows how Excel's PivotTables, PivotCharts and slicers can support hospital performance monitoring. It brings admissions, workload, billing, payment and insurance indicators into one interactive view that management can use to make informed decisions.
